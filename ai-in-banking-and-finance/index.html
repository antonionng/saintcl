Traditional ML vs Generative AI

Traditional ML

Learns patterns from structured data. Predicts, classifies, scores. Requires labelled training data. Output is a number or category.

Generative AI

Generates new content from prompts. Summarises, drafts, translates. Uses large pretrained models. Output is text, code, or media.

Prompt Engineering for Banking

Prompts are the control interface for generative AI. In banking, prompt design is a governance decision, not just a technical one.

EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

What Is Prompt Engineering?

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing inputs to AI models that produce reliable, controlled, and useful outputs.

  • Not "talking to a chatbot" but designing a control protocol
  • The prompt defines what the model should do, how, and what it must not do
  • In banking, prompts are part of the operational control framework
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Why Prompts Matter in Regulated Environments

Without Constraints

"Summarise this customer complaint." Result: may include PII, opinions, or fabricated details. Uncontrolled output.

With Constraints

"Summarise this complaint in under 100 words. Exclude account numbers. Flag severity 1-5. Do not suggest resolution." Controlled, auditable.

EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

The Anatomy of a Banking Prompt

  1. Role: Define who the model is acting as
  2. Task: State clearly what to do
  3. Context: Provide relevant background
  4. Constraints: Set boundaries on output
  5. Format: Specify output structure
  6. Guardrails: State what must not appear
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Prompt Pattern: Role-Task-Format

The most common pattern for banking applications.

You are an AJB compliance analyst. Review the following transaction summary and identify any patterns that may require further investigation under AML guidelines. Output a structured table with: transaction ID, flag reason, severity (1-5), and recommended next step. Do not make final determinations.

Prompt Pattern: Extract-Summarise-Flag

Used for document processing and review workflows.

Extract the key terms from this contract. Summarise each section in one sentence. Flag any clause that differs from AJB standard terms. Present as a numbered list. Do not provide legal advice or interpretation.

Common Prompt Failures in Banking

  • Too vague: "Analyse this data" produces unfocused, unreliable output
  • No guardrails: Model may fabricate data, cite nonexistent policies, or make decisions
  • Wrong role: Asking a model to "be a financial advisor" creates liability risk
  • No format specification: Inconsistent output breaks downstream workflows
  • Ignoring edge cases: What should the model do when data is missing or ambiguous?
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Hallucination and Fabrication

Generative AI models can produce confident, well-structured output that is factually wrong.

  • Models do not "know" facts; they predict likely text sequences
  • Fabricated policy references, invented statistics, false citations
  • In banking, a hallucinated compliance reference could cause real harm
  • Mitigation: constrained prompts, source verification, human review
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Prompt Testing and Iteration

  1. Start with a clear, constrained prompt
  2. Test with representative banking data (not toy examples)
  3. Review outputs against expected results
  4. Identify failure modes: wrong format, missing constraints, hallucinations
  5. Refine constraints and retest
  6. Document the final prompt as a controlled template
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Prompt Governance

In a regulated bank, prompts are not informal. They should be:

  • Version controlled and documented
  • Reviewed by the relevant business function
  • Tested before deployment
  • Subject to change management processes
  • Auditable: who approved this prompt, when, for what use case

Bias and Fairness

AI models can perpetuate or amplify biases present in training data.

  • Credit scoring models may discriminate based on proxies for protected characteristics
  • Customer service models may perform differently across languages or dialects
  • Recruitment screening models have shown documented bias in multiple industries
  • Fairness testing must be part of model validation, not an afterthought

Transparency

Explainability

Can we explain why a specific output was produced? Can an auditor trace the reasoning? Can a customer challenge the result?

AI Risk Assessment Framework

  1. Identify: What does this AI system do? What decisions does it inform?
  2. Assess: What is the risk if it fails? Who is affected?
  3. Classify: Low, medium, or high risk based on impact and likelihood
  4. Control: What oversight, testing, and monitoring is required?
  5. Review: How often should this assessment be repeated?
EMFT x Al Jazira Bank | AI in Banking and Finance

Data Governance for AI

  • Training data must be representative and free of prohibited biases
  • Customer data used in AI must comply with PDPL consent requirements
  • Data lineage: can you trace every AI input back to its source?
  • Data retention: AI training data has its own lifecycle requirements
  • Cross-border data: cloud-hosted AI models raise data residency questions

Model Governance

Before Deployment

Validation testing. Bias assessment. Documentation. Approval by model risk committee. Regulatory notification if required.

After Deployment

Performance monitoring. Drift detection. Periodic revalidation. Incident tracking. Decommission criteria.

Third-Party AI Risk

Most GenAI tools are provided by third-party vendors. This creates additional risk:

  • What happens if the vendor discontinues the service?

AI Capability vs AI Readiness

Capability

Can the AI do this task technically? Is the technology mature enough? Are models available?

Readiness

Is the bank ready? Do we have the data, governance, skills, and processes to deploy and manage it responsibly?

Capability without readiness leads to failed projects. Readiness without capability leads to wasted effort. Both must be assessed.
