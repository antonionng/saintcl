# Module 7: AI in Banking and Finance - Facilitator Guide

## Module Overview

Module 7 is the capstone of the AJB AI and Data Training Programme. It runs as a single intensive day (4 hours) and is designed to synthesise learning from all seven modules into a practical, strategic output: a leadership briefing recommending one AI use case for Al Jazira Bank.

This module is not primarily technical. Participants have built their technical skills in Modules 1-6. Today they apply those skills to real banking decisions, working with use case data, prompt design, risk assessment, and stakeholder communication. The output should feel like real work, not an academic exercise.

## Delivery Stance

- **Capstone energy:** This is the final module. Set the tone that this is the culmination of the programme. Participants should feel the weight of bringing everything together.
- **Programme synthesis:** Reference earlier modules throughout. When participants analyse data, note they are using Module 2-3 skills. When they build prompts, connect to Module 5 NLP concepts. Make the thread visible.
- **Strategic over technical:** Push discussions toward business decisions, not technical implementation. "Should we do this?" matters more today than "How would we build this?"
- **Balanced judgement:** Reward participants who name risks honestly and recommend conservative adoption paths. Penalise (gently) those who recommend broad AI rollout without governance.
- **AJB specificity:** Every recommendation should be about Al Jazira Bank, not banking in general. Push participants to name specific departments, processes, and stakeholders.

## Facilitation Points

### Opening (s01-s08)
- Ask 2-3 participants to name one thing they learned in an earlier module that they expect to use today. This grounds the capstone framing.
- The programme journey slide (s03) is a good moment to acknowledge how far participants have come.

### AI Landscape (s09-s26)
- The GenAI vs Traditional ML distinction (s11-s12) is critical. Many participants conflate the two. Spend time here.
- Use the "When to Use Which" table (s12) as a discussion prompt. Ask participants to add their own examples.
- The use case spectrum (s22) is the conceptual anchor for the rest of the day. Return to it often.

### Prompt Engineering (s27-s42)
- The "without constraints vs with constraints" slide (s29) is the single most important slide in this section. Let it land.
- Walk through one prompt pattern live, building it step by step rather than just showing the finished version.
- When reviewing prompt design work, look for: specific constraints, explicit prohibitions, format specifications, and edge case handling.

### Risk and Compliance (s43-s58)
- The human oversight framework (s49) should be connected to the use case spectrum from earlier.
- Participants often understate third-party risk (s53). Push on this during discussion.
- The risk assessment activity is individual first, then paired. This is deliberate: participants should form their own view before being influenced.

### Case Studio (s59-s66)
- This is the synthesis activity. Teams should select one use case and carry it through the full pipeline.
- Circulate actively. Watch for teams that are stuck on use case selection and help them choose within 5 minutes.
- The peer review (s65) is valuable but can be compressed if time is short. Minimum: 5 minutes of written feedback exchange.

### Programme Close (s75-s80)
- The final reflection (s79) should be individual and silent, then shared. Give at least 2 minutes of quiet writing time.
- Close with genuine acknowledgement. This cohort has completed a significant programme.

## Common Challenges

| Challenge | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "AI will transform everything" enthusiasm | Ask: "Which specific process, with which controls?" Redirect to the use case spectrum. |
| Vague risk assessments | Push for specific failure scenarios: "What exactly goes wrong? Who is affected? What is the cost?" |
| Prompt templates without guardrails | Ask: "What happens if the model does [undesirable thing]? Does your prompt prevent that?" |
| Recommendations without human oversight | Return to s49. Every recommendation must name the oversight level and justify it. |
| Technical depth instead of strategic thinking | Redirect: "Your audience today is the CDO and CRO, not the engineering team." |
| Teams stuck on use case selection | Suggest they pick from the top 5 in the dataset by estimated value or pick one from their own department. |
| Participants comparing to competitors | Redirect to AJB-specific context: "What matters is what is right for AJB's risk profile and maturity." |

## Assessment Guidance

This module uses four activities, each with its own rubric in the participant workbook. Overall assessment:

- **Band 4 (Distinction):** All four activities complete with stretch artefacts. Leadership briefing is presentation-ready. Risk assessment is thorough and specific. Analysis references AJB context throughout.
- **Band 3 (Merit):** All four activities complete. Work is well-structured and demonstrates clear understanding. Briefing would need minor refinement before presentation.
- **Band 2 (Pass):** All required artefacts complete. Analysis addresses core requirements. Some areas lack depth or AJB specificity.
- **Band 1 (Developing):** Some required artefacts incomplete. Analysis is generic rather than AJB-specific. Risk assessment or briefing lacks structure.

Key assessment signals:
- Does the participant distinguish between GenAI and Traditional ML appropriately?
- Are prompt constraints specific, testable, and relevant to banking?
- Does the risk assessment name concrete failure scenarios, not just risk categories?
- Is the leadership briefing structured, quantified, and actionable?
- Does the work reference or apply skills from earlier modules?

## Programme Close Standard

This is the final module. The close should:

1. Acknowledge completion of the full 7-module programme
2. Summarise the skill progression from Python basics to strategic AI assessment
3. Give participants time for individual reflection (minimum 2 minutes of quiet)
4. Invite 3-4 volunteers to share their 30-day action item
5. Thank participants genuinely and close with the message that they are now equipped to contribute to AJB's AI and data future
6. Distribute any programme completion certificates or materials
7. Collect programme feedback if applicable

## Mixed-Level Delivery Overlay
- Intro route: keep the room grounded in the core use case, prompt discipline, and visible risk controls.
- Advanced route: use stronger participants for deeper prompt iteration and richer adoption trade-off discussion.
- Because the module is short, stop any conversation that becomes too broad or speculative.

## Virtual Engagement Checkpoints
- Opening: use one myth-versus-reality prompt to align expectations quickly.
- Mid-session: stop after the first prompt critique and ask what risk remains even if the answer looks good.
- Close: ask every participant for one adoption principle they would keep visible in their role.
